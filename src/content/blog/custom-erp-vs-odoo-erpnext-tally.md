Most growing businesses in India start on Tally and spreadsheets. It works until it doesn't: stock in the godown stops matching the books, production depends on one person's memory, and the owner waits until month-end to learn what happened. That is the point where people start searching for an ERP.

You have three realistic paths:

1. **Stay on Tally** and add tools around it.
2. **Implement an open-source ERP** such as Odoo or ERPNext.
3. **Build a custom ERP** around your own process.

This guide explains what each does well, where each struggles and how to decide.

## What Tally does well, and where it stops

Tally is accounting software, and at that job it is hard to beat. Accountants across India know it, GST returns and invoicing are built in, and it handles ledgers, billing and basic inventory reliably.

It starts to strain when you need things beyond the books:

- **Production.** Bills of materials, production planning, job work and wastage tracking are limited.
- **Process across departments.** Purchase requests, approvals, quality checks and dispatch usually happen outside Tally, on paper or WhatsApp.
- **Access for outsiders.** Dealers, salespeople and customers cannot log in to place orders or check status.
- **Live visibility.** Owners often depend on someone exporting a report.

If your pain is mainly reporting or a missing link, such as orders from the field reaching accounts, you may not need a full ERP. A small custom tool that syncs with Tally can solve it.

## Odoo and ERPNext: open-source ERP platforms

Odoo and ERPNext are complete ERP platforms. Each covers accounting, inventory, purchase, sales, manufacturing, HR and more in one system.

**What they do well**

- **Broad coverage from day one.** Hundreds of standard features are already built and tested.
- **Lower starting cost than building from scratch**, when your process is close to standard.
- **Active communities and ecosystems**, with documentation, add-ons and many implementers.
- **Indian localisation**, including GST, is available for both.

**How they differ**

| | Odoo | ERPNext |
|---|---|---|
| Licence | Open-source Community edition, plus a paid Enterprise edition per user | Fully open source |
| Ecosystem | Very large library of apps and add-ons | Smaller and more uniform |
| Typical fit | Businesses wanting many functions, including website and e-commerce, in one suite | Manufacturers, traders and service firms wanting a lean, open system |
| Hosting | Vendor cloud or your own server | Vendor cloud or your own server |

**Where they struggle**

- **Heavy customisation.** The further your process is from the standard flow, the more custom code you need, and upgrades become harder with every change.
- **Complexity.** A platform built for every industry shows staff many screens and fields they will never use, which slows adoption.
- **Implementation risk.** Most failed ERP projects fail at setup and training, not at the software.

## What a custom ERP gives you

A custom ERP is software written for one business: yours.

- **It follows your workflow.** Screens appear in the order your staff work, using your own terms.
- **Only what you need.** Nothing unused to learn or click past.
- **No per-user licence.** You pay to build and host it, not for each login.
- **It changes with you.** New product lines, branches or approval rules become part of the software.
- **You own it.** The code and data are yours.

The trade-offs are real. A custom ERP takes longer to deliver than configuring a platform, needs a development partner for changes, and everything, including the standard features, has to be built or integrated.

## Five questions that decide it

| Question | Points to Tally plus tools | Points to Odoo or ERPNext | Points to custom ERP |
|---|---|---|---|
| What hurts most? | Reports or one missing link | Many departments on separate tools | A core process no product handles |
| How standard is your process? | Standard trading | Mostly standard | Unusual or a competitive advantage |
| How many users? | A few | A moderate number | Many, or growing quickly |
| Who needs access? | Accounts team | Internal staff | Staff, dealers, vendors and customers |
| How often does the process change? | Rarely | Sometimes | Often |

No column wins every time. Many businesses end up with a mix: an open-source ERP at the core with a few custom modules, or a custom operations system with accounts kept in Tally.

## What actually drives the cost

Whichever path you choose, the software itself is rarely the largest cost. These are:

- **Scope.** The number of modules, users, branches and reports.
- **Customisation.** Every change from standard behaviour.
- **Data migration.** Cleaning and importing items, parties, opening balances and stock.
- **Integrations.** Tally, e-invoicing, e-way bills, payment gateways, WhatsApp, barcode hardware.
- **Training and change.** Getting staff to use the system every day.

Ask every vendor to quote these separately. A low licence price with open-ended customisation is not a low price.

## How to avoid a failed ERP project

1. **Write down the process first.** Walk through one order from enquiry to payment and note every step, document and approval.
2. **Go live in phases.** Start with the module that hurts most, such as stock or dispatch, then add the rest.
3. **Clean your data** before migration. Duplicate items and parties carry every old problem into the new system.
4. **Name one owner** inside the business who can make decisions and chase adoption.
5. **Train on real work**, with the screens people will use daily, not a generic demo.
