For many businesses, especially in India, the UAE and across Asia, WhatsApp is where customers actually talk. Enquiries arrive at all hours, the same questions repeat dozens of times a day, and important conversations get buried on one employee's phone.

Automation fixes this, but only if it's set up the right way. Here's what you can automate, what you need, and the rules to respect.

## WhatsApp Business App vs WhatsApp Business API

There are two different products, and the difference matters.

**WhatsApp Business App** is the free app on a phone. It offers a business profile, quick replies, labels and simple away messages. It works for a solo owner, but it is tied to a phone and cannot connect properly to your CRM or support multiple agents at scale.

**WhatsApp Business Platform (API)** is what real automation runs on. It allows:

- Multiple team members on one number
- Integration with your CRM, website and other systems
- Chatbots and AI assistants
- Approved template messages for notifications and follow-ups
- Conversation history stored in your own systems

Access is provided through Meta directly or through an approved Business Solution Provider. Conversations are billed by Meta according to its current pricing, which varies by country and message category.

## Seven things worth automating

### 1. Instant replies to new enquiries

Speed wins leads. An automated first reply can greet the customer, answer common questions and collect key details such as name, requirement, budget and location within seconds, at any hour.

### 2. Lead capture straight into your CRM

Every new WhatsApp conversation can create or update a lead in your CRM automatically, tagged with its source. No more copying numbers from phones into spreadsheets.

### 3. AI assistants for common questions

AI models can answer questions about products, pricing, availability and policies using your own approved information. Done well, the assistant hands over to a human when a question is complex, sensitive, or the customer asks for a person.

### 4. Appointment booking and reminders

Clinics, salons, consultants and real-estate teams can let customers pick a slot inside WhatsApp, then send automatic confirmations and reminders to reduce no-shows.

### 5. Order and payment updates

E-commerce and service businesses can send order confirmations, shipping updates, payment links and receipts as approved template messages.

### 6. Follow-up sequences

If a lead goes quiet, timed follow-ups (using approved templates) can bring the conversation back, while stopping automatically once the customer replies.

### 7. Routing to the right person

Conversations can be routed by language, location, product or team, so the right person answers and managers can see response times.

## Rules you must follow

WhatsApp is strict about quality, and breaking the rules can get your number restricted.

- **Get opt-in.** Message customers only when they have agreed to hear from you on WhatsApp.
- **Use approved templates** for business-initiated messages outside the customer service window.
- **Respect the 24-hour window.** Free-form replies are allowed within 24 hours of the customer's last message; after that, use templates.
- **Make it easy to stop.** Honour opt-out requests immediately.
- **Avoid spam.** Low-quality or unwanted messages lower your quality rating and messaging limits.
- **Protect personal data** in line with local privacy laws such as India's DPDP Act or the UAE PDPL.

## Where AI helps and where it doesn't

AI is excellent at understanding free-text questions, summarising conversations and drafting replies. It should not invent prices, make commitments your business can't keep, or handle sensitive decisions without review. The best setups combine AI for speed with clear rules and human handover for anything important.

## How to get started

1. **List your top 20 customer questions** and the answers you want given.
2. **Map your lead journey:** what happens from first message to sale.
3. **Choose your systems:** which CRM or database should store contacts and conversations.
4. **Start small:** automate first replies and lead capture, measure, then add AI and follow-ups.
