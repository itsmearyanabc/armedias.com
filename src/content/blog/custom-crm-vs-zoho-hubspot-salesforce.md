Every growing sales team hits the same moment: leads are scattered across WhatsApp chats, spreadsheets and inboxes, follow-ups depend on memory, and nobody can say with confidence how much is in the pipeline. The answer is a CRM. The harder question is **which kind**.

You have two broad options:

1. **Off-the-shelf CRM** such as Zoho CRM, HubSpot or Salesforce, configured for your business.
2. **Custom CRM** built specifically around your sales process.

Neither is always right. This guide walks through how to decide.

## What an off-the-shelf CRM does well

Platforms like Zoho, HubSpot and Salesforce have spent years refining the basics of contact management, deal pipelines, email tracking and reporting.

- **Fast to start.** You can sign up today and have a working pipeline within days.
- **Proven features.** Email sequences, mobile apps, dashboards and permission systems are already built and tested.
- **Large ecosystems.** Marketplaces offer integrations with accounting, telephony, marketing and support tools.
- **Predictable upgrades.** New features arrive without you paying for development.

If your sales process is close to a standard B2B pipeline (lead → qualified → proposal → won/lost), an off-the-shelf CRM with good configuration is often the right first step.

## Where off-the-shelf CRMs start to hurt

Problems usually appear as a business grows or when its process is unusual.

### Per-user pricing grows with your team

Most CRMs charge per user, per month, and the features growing teams need (automation, advanced reporting, custom objects, API access) often sit in higher tiers. Ten users on a mid-tier plan becomes fifty users on an enterprise plan, and the annual bill grows with every hire.

### Your process doesn't fit the template

Real estate brokerages track properties, site visits and channel partners. Education businesses track counselling sessions, applications and fee instalments. Clinics track appointments and treatments. You can bend a generic CRM to fit, but every workaround adds friction, and frustrated teams stop entering data.

### Integration limits

Connecting a CRM to WhatsApp, a local payment gateway, your ERP or an industry portal can require paid add-ons, middleware, or may not be possible at the depth you need.

### You rent, you don't own

Your data lives on the vendor's platform under the vendor's pricing and terms. Moving away later means export, cleanup and migration.

## What a custom CRM gives you

A custom CRM is software built around how *your* team actually works.

- **Exact fit.** Pipelines, fields, screens and automations match your process instead of the other way round.
- **No per-seat fees.** You pay to build and host it, not for every new salesperson.
- **Deep integrations.** WhatsApp Business API, payment gateways, telephony, accounting and lead portals can be connected directly.
- **Full ownership.** The code and data are yours, hosted where you choose, including in your own region for data-protection requirements.
- **Room to grow.** Add modules such as quotations, invoicing, inventory or a customer portal as the business evolves.

The trade-offs are real: a custom CRM takes weeks to build rather than days, needs a development partner for changes, and has an upfront cost.

## A simple way to decide

Ask these five questions:

| Question | Points to off-the-shelf | Points to custom |
|---|---|---|
| How standard is your sales process? | Standard B2B pipeline | Industry-specific steps and records |
| How many users in two years? | A handful | Dozens or more |
| How important are WhatsApp and local integrations? | Nice to have | Core to how you sell |
| Do you need data in a specific country? | No requirement | Yes |
| Will the CRM grow into other operations? | Sales only | Quotes, billing, inventory, portals |

If most answers fall in the left column, start with Zoho, HubSpot or Salesforce and invest in proper setup. If most fall on the right, a custom CRM will usually pay for itself in fit, adoption and long-term cost.

## The middle path: customised platforms

There is a third option many businesses overlook: keep an off-the-shelf CRM as the core and build **custom extensions** around it. This might be a WhatsApp integration, a lead-routing engine, a customer portal or reporting that pulls from several systems. You keep the platform's proven features and fill the gaps that matter.

## What to do next

Before choosing any CRM, write down your sales process step by step: where leads come from, who touches them, what information you need at each stage and which reports leadership wants each week. That one document will make any CRM project faster and cheaper, whether you buy or build.
